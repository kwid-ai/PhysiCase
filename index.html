PhysiCase — Dual-Layer Validation of Synthetic Cases Using Generative AI
A reproducible pipeline combining automated LLM-based screening with expert educator review to generate high-quality synthetic case studies
Authors: Oyindolapo Komolafe, Angela Roberts, Jacob Shelley, Andrews Tawiah
Research team affiliated with Ontario, Canada universities  •  Domain scope - Physiotherapy MSK cases  •  github.com/kwid-ai/PhysiCase
The Problem
Privacy barriers restrict access to real patient cases for physiotherapy education
Existing case banks prohibit AI training and algorithmic generation
Synthetic data bridges this gap — but requires rigorous validation before educational use
RQ1 — Educational validity
Do synthetic MSK cases exhibit clinical plausibility and pedagogical utility?
RQ2 — Metric alignment
Do automated metrics correlate with expert clinical judgment?

4Cross-Layer Alignment: AI vs. Expert
Spearman correlations between automated (DeepEval) and expert ratings reveal what AI can and cannot predict:
↗
Completeness → Usability ρ = 0.39
Having all required sections predicts student-facing usefulness — completeness improves teachability
→
Prompt alignment → Fidelity ρ = weak
Following instructions ≠ clinical coherence; a case can be well-formatted but clinically inconsistent
↘
Answer relevance → Medical accuracy ρ = 0.01
On-topic text does NOT equal clinically safe content; "surface relevance ≠ clinical safety"
Key Finding
Automated metrics efficiently reduce expert burden but cannot replace clinical judgment for detecting subtle reasoning errors
5Expert-Identified Failure Modes
Failure Mode 1
Reductive Logic
LLMs assign NPRS pain scores to single muscles — clinically inaccurate. Pain is multidimensional, not anatomically pinpoint.
"Specific NPRS to a specific muscle is extremely problematic"
Failure Mode 2
Biomechanical Inconsistency
Mismatches between injury mechanism, presentation, and assistive devices. Patients "in great shape" using canes; athletes delaying care illogically.
"Mechanism is problematic, imaging is problematic"
Failure Mode 3
Administrative/Contextual Gaps
Missing WSIB/insurance context, occupational status, and functional indicators (e.g., BMI instead of obesity) that are critical for real-world physiotherapy reasoning.
What this means for learners
These nuance gaps risk teaching flawed clinical abstractions — undetectable by automated metrics alone
6Key Takeaways
✓
Dual-layer validation works — automated screening efficiently filters structurally weak cases, preserving expert time for nuance
✓
Frontier LLMs can meet educational needs — usability (97.5% CVI) and realism (95% CVI) indicate real pedagogical potential
✓
GPT-4.1 is the recommended model for high-stakes MSK education; smaller models (Llama 4 Scout) are not yet suitable for direct student use
!
Medical accuracy is the critical gap — experts remain essential; automated metrics cannot detect subtle clinical reasoning failures
!
Common conditions generate better — lower-body orthopedic cases outperform complex/systemic conditions like fibromyalgia or low back pain
7Limitations & Future Work
Limitations
Small pilot (30 expert-reviewed cases); imbalanced model sampling; Ontario-only educators; ceiling effects in IRR
Future directions
Scale generation; clinical coherence gates; expanded expert panels; reasoning-sensitive automated rubrics
